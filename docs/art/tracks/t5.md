# T5 Heist: the heist, the replay and the result

Branch `claude/3d-screens`. Owns `client/world/world_view.gd`, `actor_builder.gd`, the new `client/world/fx_*.gd` (and the designs' fonts in `client/world/fx_fonts/`), and the heist, HUD, replay and result screens. T6 (`claude/3d-screens-world`) owns the other realm screens and calls `WorldView`'s public API, which stays additive: every screen that sets no `follow` keeps the old fixed framing.

## Hooks outside the track's files

- `client/scripts/settings.gd`, `settings_screen.gd`: one switch, `camera_buttons` (§21.1: "a Settings switch shows rotate and zoom buttons"), saved with the others and listed with the other toggles.
- `client/i18n/strings.csv`: six rows for that switch and its four buttons' names, in every language column.
- `client/tests/run.gd`: one line registering `HeistFxTests`.

## What is built

**The heist screen** (`client/scripts/heist_screen.gd`, `heist_hud.gd`): the world fills the screen and the HUD floats over it, laid out as §21.8 and the Screens v3 heist have it.
- Top, three columns: the law banner (a chip a standing pylon, with its school's shape); the clock in a Lilita One pill counting down, turning red and pulsing once a second in its last 30 seconds, with the alarm's six bells beneath (amber to three, red from four, shaking when it rises) and "Alarm N / 6"; the loot with its two numbers and two bars (the bags' fill beside a sack, the Vault's cracking beside a lock that opens).
- Bottom: the hint on a glass pill; Settings' turn and zoom buttons when switched on; the orders (Pause in a rehearsal, Halt and Drop as round icon buttons named in words, the gait with its footprints); the crew's portraits (`FxPortrait`: face, health ring, bag, role letters on a tag, a gold ring and a lift when chosen, a red disc when down); a big red Get out with its skiff. Left-handed play mirrors both rows.
- The HUD is two columns, one hung from the top and one standing on the bottom (`FxSkin.frame`), so 200% text never pushes a control off the screen. Every control, string and screen-reader name is kept.
- Surfaces are §21.8's: dark glass at 78%, violet cards, parchment with brown ink, ink outlines of 1.5 points (3 in high contrast), a gloss and a drop shadow (`FxSkin`, `FxGloss`). The fonts are the designs' Lilita One and Manrope (OFL, Latin subsets, with the client's font behind them).
- Grabbed loot flies from the crew into the loot panel (§21.8: "rewards fly into their counter").
- The landing picker is a parchment card; the camera leans toward the spot on show, which a bobbing gold pin marks, and lifts the realm above the card.

**The world** (`client/world/world_view.gd`):
- The heist's follow camera: 50° pitch, 12 tiles across round the chosen crew member, 8 close up, the whole realm zoomed out (§21.1). It eases after the crew and frames them a little above the middle. On landing it drops from the whole realm to the crew in 1.2 seconds.
- Input: a tap is `tapped` (never part of a pinch or a twist) and answers with a ring on the tile; a long press is `held` and shows every crew member's name; a double tap steps the zoom; a pinch zooms smoothly; a twist turns a quarter; a mouse wheel zooms. No action needs two fingers.
- A gentle shake (never past §21.8's 4 pixels, none with reduced motion), and §21.1's one-second swoop between realms: out and back in, with picture-book clouds rolling across the rift's violet (a cut with reduced motion).
- Actors: a root the simulation places, a `Rig` the animation bobs, leans and knocks over, a `Figure` under it (body and two arms that swing) and `Feet` that step in turn. A character model replaces the `Figure`. Figures tiptoe crouched, lean running, breathe standing and lie down when down.
- Guard sight (§21.12): the shown guard's tiles as `seen(i)` gives them, flat, sharp-edged, with a darker line round the shape; every other guard on its feet shows a dashed outline of its cone. The guard shown is the selected one or, as the designs focus it, the most aware (the nearest to the chosen crew member breaks a tie). A ring under each guard in its state's colour, each crew member's in its role's colour, a pulsing gold one under the chosen.
- The chosen crew member's last order as the designs' route preview: ink dots from it to where it was sent, white where they cross the shown guard's red sight, and a ring at the end. It draws the order, not a path the client works out.

**World-anchored HUD** (`fx_anchors.gd`): health and bag bars over the crew; a gold chevron over the chosen one; spinning stars over one that is down; over each guard a quiet pip, or a speech bubble with "?", "??" or "!" drawn as shapes, filled white, yellow, orange or red by awareness; "z" and stars over a knocked-out guard; the VAULT tag with its cracking bar; each pylon's school shape over it; pop-up numbers; names on a long press; a soft ink shade at the top and bottom of a heist behind the HUD; the alarm as a red tint at the screen's edge, rising with the alarm and flashing on an alert (a screen-space tint, never over the middle).

**What happens** (`fx_events.gd`): each change between two readings of `RulebornReplay` is drawn, so a replay shows the same effects every time: loot (gathered into one "+N" every 12 ticks a member, with coins), hits, a crew member going down (stars, a shake) and being revived (a green ring and a heart), a guard knocked out ("KO" and stars) or alerted (a red ring and the edge's flash), the alarm going up (the bells ring), a pylon breaking (shards in its school's colour and a shock ring), a ward going down, the Vault opening (coins and a gold ring), a crossing (the swoop).

**Laws on screen** (`fx_laws.gd`): every launch law (the 24 `launch: true` laws) has a world signature: motes (a `MultiMesh` moved on the CPU, one draw call) and, for most, a mark on the ground (cracks, a hex sheen, ice, a spiral, glass panes, aurora bands, arcs, rings, Kinship's live green threads between neighbours). Each law bends the figures' steps: rate, hop, float and lean (§21.7's movement signatures). Glowing dark dims the sun and the sky's light. Reduced motion slows the motes and drops the leaning. At most two draw calls a law.

**The replay** (`replay_screen.gd`): the same full-screen world and HUD, the camera following the crew, playback controls in the heist's skin, a progress bar, a tap on a guard shows its sight.

**The result** (`result_screen.gd`): the island after the heist, the replay played to its end and seen whole from the home pitch, swaying slowly; the outcome on parchment with the grade in Lilita One; when the heist paid, gold rays rise behind the card and coins fly from the island into it.

## Still worse than the design

- The ground and the island are T2's and T3's: a pale checkerboard here against the design's painted grass and paths. In grayscale the design's ground is dark with light paths; ours is light all over, which flattens the HUD's contrast against it.
- The figures are the diorama's chibis; the design's heist has sculpted creatures (T1).
- No Sage, cast or ability portraits: the build's heist has no abilities yet, so the portraits show health, bag and state, not uses.
- The guard's suspicion fill does not travel from its eyes to the crew (§21.12): the replay gives a guard's state, not how far its noticing has gone.
- Guard sight is one flat red; the design tiers it by distance, which the replay does not give either.
