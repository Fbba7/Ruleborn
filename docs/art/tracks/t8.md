# T8 Law effects

Branch `claude/3d-law-fx`. Owns `client/world/law_fx*.gd` and `law_fx*.gdshader`, the shots `client/tests/shots/law_*.gd`, and the law card, Weaving and Harmony screens.

## What is built

§21.7's laws on screen, drawn from the simulation's truth. A replay looks the same every time it is played.

- **`LawFx`** (`client/world/law_fx.gd`) is a `Node3D`.
  - `LawFx.attach(view, replay)` builds every woven law's world signature over the realm, and from then on keeps itself in step with that replay every frame. Integration into the heist and replay screens is this one line, after `view.show_replay(replay)`; `law_heist_screen.gd` shows it on the real heist screen without editing it. It reads `replay.pylons()` (each pylon's `law` and `school`) and the grid (`size()`, `features()`, `heights()`, wards, guard posts), so a Glass realm's clear walls are its own walls and a Slow rivers realm's syrup is its own water.
  - It adds itself under T5's `view.effects` when that hook exists, else under `view.world`.
  - `sync(tick)` moves everything from the heist's ticks, in the shaders.
  - `sync_world(replay)` fades a broken pylon's signature (§7.1) and moves the pieces that follow characters.
  - `gait(law)` returns the movement signature as numbers. `crown(school)` returns a pylon crown mesh. `budget(law)` returns a signature's triangles and draw calls. `set_paradox(id)` adds a paradox overlay.
- **What a signature is made of** (§21.3; there is no full-screen post-processing):
  - `law_fx_mote.gdshader`: camera-facing motes, a whole field of them in one mesh and one draw call. There are 12 hand-drawn shapes: dot, inked dot, sparkle, streak, Z, petal, ring, hexagon, zigzag spark, drop, leaf and wisp. Every move is a function of the tick.
  - `law_fx_decal.gdshader`: ground marks as flat tile quads. There are 17 procedural patterns, including ice gloss, hexagon sheen, cracks, a spiral toward the Heart, delay ripples, aurora ribbons, creeping moss, glowing runes, pools of light, dream haze, static crackle, a clock face, racing cloud shadows and syrup. They draw under the guard's sight overlay, so §21.12's red tiles always read.
  - `law_fx_solid.gdshader` and `law_fx_glass.gdshader`: toon-lit and see-through pieces, moved in the vertex stage by `law_fx_motion.gdshaderinc`. The moves are hover, time-lapse growth, jelly wobble, hum, sway, orbit, float, tremble and drop. `law_fx_ink.gdshader` is §21.3's 1.5-pixel ink outline that follows the moving pieces.
  - `law_fx_thread.gdshader`: lines between points. These are harmony threads, dissonance jags, chord edges, lightning bolts with a white-hot core, and wavy wind lines.
  - `law_fx_tint.gdshader`: a screen-space tint on one quad before the camera. It gives a vignette, a wash or a flash.
  - Light: Day and night turns the realm's sun and ambient light, and Glowing dark dims them. Both are restored when the effects leave the tree.
- **The 24 launch laws** (`law_fx_signs.gd`, `law_fx_signs2.gd`):
  - Ported from the diorama's `lawFX`: Stillness, Slow rivers, Half gravity, Current, Day and night, Frictionless, Glass, Bloom and Glowing dark.
  - Designed from §21.7's words: the other 15 launch laws.
  - The 24 laws after launch have lighter signatures of their own (`law_fx_signs3.gd`), so a law added later needs no art pass. Any other law falls back to its school's motes round its pylon.
- **Harmony, dissonance and chord effects** (`law_fx_links.gd`, `law_fx_harmony.gd`):
  - Links follow §21.7's words: a thread in the note's colour between two pylons, a jagged flickering line for a dissonance, and a slowly turning triangle of light between three crowns for a chord. Light runs along each of them.
  - Each note's harmony effect (8) is drawn round both pylons: wind lines, sinking anchors, light pools 2 tiles wider, hush pools, spiralling leaves, sparks, repeating rings, mask shimmer.
  - The 4 dissonances are gusts, flicker, wildfire and déjà vu.
  - Each of the 12 chords has its own overlay.
  - A note's symbol (§8.1) travels along its thread as a shape, so a thread is never read by colour alone (§20.8).
  - The notes and chords are copied from `content/`, and a test checks the copy.
- **Paradoxes** (§7.5):
  - Reversal: dust falls upward, and violet creeps in at the screen's edges.
  - Twin suns: a second, shadowless sun and two sun discs.
  - Unmaking: torn scraps flutter and every signature stutters.
- **Pylon crowns** (`law_fx_crown.gd`): each school's shape from §21.2, in the school's colour with its shadow tint, lit from within, on a collar. The shapes are Motion's chevron and Energy's bolt (both extruded outlines), Matter's cube on its corner, Time's framed hourglass, Life's leaf and Spirit's eye. A pool of the school's colour lies under each pylon, so a signature reads as its pylon's.
  - **For T3:** `LawFxCrown.mesh(school)` (or `LawFx.crown(school)`) is a static, cached `ArrayMesh` in Geo's vertex colours. Its glow comes through the alpha channel, so `toon.gdshader` with `glow_from_alpha` lights it. `PieceKit.pylon()` can append it at `LawFxCrown.HEIGHT`, at 1.5× scale, in place of its ball.
- **Movement signatures** (`law_fx_gait.gd`):
  - `LawFx.gait(law)` gives every law and paradox as a dictionary with fixed keys: `bounce`, `squash`, `lean` and `lean_to`, `stride`, `tempo`, `float`, `skate`, `tiptoe`, `crouch`, `sway`, `trail`, `shimmer`, `arms`, `rhythm`, `who` and `extra`. Each key is documented in the file.
  - `LawFxGait.blend(laws)` layers several laws into one gait.
  - `LawFxWalker.pose(node, gait, t, moving)` is a reference implementation T1's `CreatureModel` can call on its body node.
- **Screens:**
  - Law card (`law_screen.gd`): a live preview (`LawFxPreview`) shows the law woven on a small realm in daylight, with its signature, its crown and a figure walking its four-second loop in the law's gait (§21.7).
  - Weaving: the preview shows the realm the weave tapped once would change, with that law added, so its harmonies and clashes appear as threads before it is woven.
  - Harmony: the preview shows the realm with the most laws and its links.
  - The previews take no taps and add no strings.

## Budgets

Measured by `LawFx.budget` and `law_fx_tests.gd`, over the tests' realm (11×11, two hedges, a wall, a tree, a pond, a lantern, a static stone, two guards, a ward). Every signature is held to at most 6,000 triangles and 8 draw calls. Most take 2–4 draw calls and a few hundred triangles. The heaviest are Glowing dark (≈4,100 triangles: its mushrooms), Half gravity (≈2,800: hovering pebbles and islets) and Density (≈2,000: tall tufts). A crown is 30–250 triangles in one draw.

The whole heist still with three laws woven (`law_heist.gd`: Haste, Static and Aurora, with a Spark harmony and the Tempest chord) renders in 83 draw calls and about 25,000 triangles, island included.

## Choices made

- **Determinism.** No particle system is used. GPUParticles and CPUParticles run on their own clocks, so a replay would differ. Every mote is a quad whose path is a function of the tick and of numbers drawn from the heist's seed.
- **When the simulation picks the moment.** For a Flicker's bright-or-dark, which law Unmaking switches off, or when Tempest's lightning strikes, the simulation exposes no state. The look is drawn without claiming the pick: a strobe, a stutter, a flash on the 20-second beat. Once the simulation names these (a hook on `RulebornReplay`), they should follow it.
- **Ground marks share the ground.** When two or three laws lay marks over the whole realm, each is drawn fainter (0.72, then 0.55), so the ground and the sight overlay still read.
- **Glass.** T3's walls stay opaque underneath, and Glass draws a clear case with glowing coloured edges over each wall. Making the walls themselves clear needs a material switch on T3's hero layer.

Integration stays one line after `show_replay`.
  - **Recommendation for the integrator:** replace FxLaws' look with LawFx (it covers all 48 laws, the links and the paradoxes), and keep FxLaws' movement, or move it onto `LawFx.gait`.
- **T3's pylons.** `PieceKit.pylon(school)` now carries its own small spinning school shape, so LawFx no longer adds its crown over it. It keeps the school-coloured pool under each pylon. `LawFxCrown.mesh` remains available if T3 wants the larger, inked crowns.

## Rounds

Each round followed the same steps: render, judge at phone size and as a grayscale thumbnail, write down the worst three problems, then fix them.

1. **Bloom, large.** Crowns did not read, motes were 2–3 pixels across, and sprouts looked like eyes. Fixed with a global mote size, crowns at 1.5×, and leafy sprouts with five-petal flowers.
2. **All 24.**
   - Ten signatures were near-invisible because they were pastel on cream ground: value contrast failed, and threads were about a pixel wide. Fixed with mid-dark saturated colours, threads 2× wider, and inked shapes.
   - Thorns' vine rings were solid discs. They are now rings of segments.
   - Kinship's vines hid inside the hedges. They now run over the tops.
3. **The heist with three laws.** Stacked ground marks went muddy, and chord edges glared. Ground marks now share the ground, and chord edges are soft.
4. **At phone proportions.** Chain lightning, Whispers and Soul swap were still faint. Arcs are now white-hot bolts with a glow, Whispers has wavy wind lines, and Soul swap's wisps are larger. Aurora's bands were all violet and now alternate.
5. **Dissonance and the paradoxes.**
   - Dissonance lines vanished on the ground. They now have an ink core.
   - Twin suns' second light washed the toon ramp out. The two suns are now drawn, not lit.
   - Reversal's and Unmaking's motes are bolder.
6. **Close-ups.** My guessed wall sizes floated Glass cases and Thorns rings off the walls. They are now measured from the realm kit. Echoes' ghosts are smoother and fuller.
7. **Crowns.** The chevron read as a "V" and the bolt as a blob. Both are now extruded outlines, the hourglass has a frame, and the collars are smaller.
8. **The real screens.** The law card preview was dark (night by the clock) and too far away, so it is now daylight and close-up. At 200% text the preview pushed Harmony's buttons off the phone, so each preview now sits in its screen's scroll.

9. **At night.** Unshaded ground marks stayed daylight-bright at 22:00 and looked pasted on. They now take the realm's light, unless they glow by nature.
10. **On the integrated branch.** Tempest's chord edges glared white over the darker grass, so they are softer. Regrowth vanished on the green ground. Crowns doubled T3's new pylon tops.

## For T5 and T6: wiring LawFx in

The API is stable, and LawFx needs nothing else from the view.

- **Attach once.** Call `var fx := LawFx.attach(view, replay)` after `view.show_replay(replay)`, and again whenever the realm changes (a new replay or another realm). On every call:
  - free the old LawFx first;
  - the attach adds the new one under `view.effects` if it exists, else under `view.world`.
- **Sync: nothing to call.** Once attached, LawFx keeps itself in step every frame:
  - between ticks it draws on from the last tick;
  - on a new tick it reads the pylons (a broken pylon's signature fades) and the characters (Soul swap's wisps, Kinship's threads);
  - where the replay never ticks (a realm screen, a paused rehearsal), its own clock runs on, so the realm stays alive.
  - `fx.sync(tick)` and `fx.sync_world(replay)` stay public, for a screen that moves the replay itself.
- **T5's FxLaws.** The attach hides `view.laws`' motes, and FxLaws still bends the walk.
  - To retire FxLaws' per-law look, drop its motes and keep its `movement`. Better still, move the walk onto `LawFxCreature.apply(model, laws, seconds, moving)` and have Cast call it after placing each figure.
- **What stays readable.**
  - Ground marks draw under the sight overlay (render priority −2).
  - Motes and threads are see-through and small.
  - Nothing is drawn over guards' marks, the Vault tag or the HUD.
  - Glowing dark dims the sun and ambient light, and restores both when LawFx leaves the tree.
- **Cost.** A signature is 2–4 draw calls and a few hundred triangles, at most 6,000 and 8.
- **Movement on characters.** `LawFxCreature.apply(c, laws, seconds, moving)` poses a `CreatureModel`'s own body without moving the model off its place. It handles bounce scaled to the character's height, lean, sway, crouch, float, the slide or leap clip a gait asks for, and the clip's speed. `LawFxCreature.rest(c)` undoes it. `law_gaits.gd` renders T1's characters under seven laws (`docs/art/shots/t8/gaits.png`).

## The third integration

LawFx is now live everywhere `WorldView` draws a realm. T5 calls `LawFx.attach(self, replay)` from `WorldView` itself and syncs it each tick, behind the static switch `WorldView.law_effects`. FxLaws keeps only the movement signatures.

- `LawFx.attach` now returns the view's own `law_fx` if it already draws that replay, so a second caller never doubles the signatures.
- `heist_screen_bloom.png` shows the real heist screen with T1's creatures and LawFx through T5's own wiring.

### For T2

On the integrated look, dimming the sun to 0.35 and the sky ambient to 0.4 hardly darkens the island's ground. Its material seems to carry much of its own light. Glowing dark and Day and night's dark phase therefore lean on LawFx's screen tint, a vignette plus a light wash.

A hook in T2's look would let a law dim the ground's own light too, for example a `night` amount on the island material.

### Reduced motion (§20.8)

With `Settings.reduced_motion` on:

- No law strobes or blinks. The Glow–Hush flicker settles in and lifts. The flashes of Tempest and Sunburst swell softly to at most a fifth. Unmaking's stutter becomes one quiet gap.
- Dissonance lines and bolts stop flickering, and Static's crackle jumps once every two seconds.
- Every signature runs at 0.6 pace.

A test covers it.
