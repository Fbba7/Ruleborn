## What cannot be settled from inside the repository

Everything above is arithmetic against the design document or a measurement the simulation harness can make. These are not: each needs a number from the outside world, and re-reading the document will not produce it. Each question is written to stand alone.

**C12 — The high revenue scenario quietly uses a different commission rate**  
*significant, proposed.* What are the current Apple and Google commission tiers and the revenue thresholds that move a developer between them, and what does that do to a revenue band either side of the boundary? §22.2’s high scenario is computed at a different rate from the other two and §22.5 does not say so.

**C20 — The 48 law sound signatures are six school sounds repeated**  
*significant, fixed.* What does a per-school sound set cost against a per-law one, and do players in comparable games actually distinguish audio cues at that granularity? §21.8 now claims six school signatures where it once claimed 48.

**C29 — v3.0 removed the playtesters and kept two phase gates that require them**  
*blocking, open.* How do small teams recruit ten to twenty playtesters for a pre-release mobile build, what does it cost, and what consent and privacy position does handling pre-release content with outside testers require? §26.2 gates two phases on those people and §25.4 says they do not exist.

**D6 — A launch-scope cut of roughly 40% is proposed**  
*change, proposed.* Which of a launch content set actually drives retention in this genre: breadth of distinct rules, or depth in a few? The proposed cut halves the laws and keeps the Island Workshop, and nothing outside this repository has been consulted about that trade.

**D9 — Iteration is given a budget**  
*change, proposed.* What is the reference-class ratio of iteration work to first-build work for a novel genre blend with no reference implementation? §26.8 estimates ~270 agent runs to build and then states that the iteration needed to find the fun is unbudgeted.

**F10 — Sixteen of forty-eight laws are parameter changes; the rest need an engine feature**  
*blocking, open.* What does each kind of law effect really cost to build — a parameter change against a spawned entity against a periodic rule — on a deterministic fixed-tick mobile simulation? `scope_cut.py` weights them 0 / 3 / 2 by judgement, and the ordering is what the conclusion rests on.

**F13 — §12.22’s matchmaking guarantee has no targets to draw from early**  
*blocking, open.* How do comparable games fill a matchmaking band when the candidate pool cannot supply it — bots, staged targets, widening, or something else? §12.22 promises a median target in a band that does not exist early.

**G11 — Localisation of a coined vocabulary has no owner**  
*minor, open.* What does it cost to localise a coined vocabulary — roughly 130 invented proper nouns — into twelve languages, and who normally owns that work on a team of this size? §26.3 assigns nobody.

**Q5 — Accept the launch-scope cut?**  
*choice, open.* How much content do comparable mobile builders actually ship at launch, counted the way §26.1 counts — distinct rules, creature forms, building types, languages — and how much did they add per season afterwards? §26.1 asks a team of one designer, one coding agent and three contractors for 48 laws, 144 creature forms, 39 terrain features and 12 languages.

**Q6 — Casual pacing: leave it, or add the catch-up discount?**  
*choice, open.* How do comparable games handle a player who is years behind the curve, and does a visible, openly labelled catch-up discount hold up in practice? §27.5 settles on one and never specifies it, against a finishing time of roughly 1,343 days.

**Q7 — The document's own open list**  
*choice, open.* Trade-mark clearance for the name, in the launch territories. §26.9 makes it a gate on any public use and it costs nothing to start.
