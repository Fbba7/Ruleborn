T5.5

Specify pathfinding and crew control before building it

Under way from here

The largest gap and the system most likely to fail §30.7's premise 3 — that delegated control still feels like agency. Drafted in spec/movement.md; make felt control a gate on the slice, not a polish item.

Not blocked, and still not yet

These look available. Starting any of them now spends work that a decision above will throw away.

  • Gameplay code — The determinism spike gates it. Anti-cheat, replay validation, free analytics, rehearsals, balance bots and the whole cost model fail together if it does not pass.
  • Realm design — The most blocked of the three design areas, and eight of its nine gates are this session's generator work: how many wards a realm holds (F26), where its defence starts (F27), one guard post or two (F22), what hold rate a generated realm must reach (F9), what the generator optimises for (F11), the eight laws whose effect is an act of authorship (F12), what a ward does at each level (G3), whether a pylon shows its law (G5), and how many producers a realm holds (C19).
  • Character art — Two things, and neither is the art. C6 — the two rosters disagree about eleven of the eighteen creatures, not about eleven wordings — and the form-notes decision in stage 4, which changes the quantity threefold: 144 forms against 54, the largest single art number in the project (D6, Q5).
  • Detailed screen design — C6, which roster the character screens read from; C3, how many law ranks the Codex displays; and C8, whether the Library is a seventeenth building with a screen of its own. An earlier version of this line named two more items — the detection model and the defence budget — and v3.0 closed both, so screen design is less blocked than this page said.
  • Localisation — Twelve languages against a coined vocabulary is a large translation problem with no owner in the team model, and §20.6 already says two at launch (G11).
  • Claiming the name publicly — §26.9 makes trademark clearance a gate, and it has not been done.
