## 1. Summary

- **The art is code, and that is good news.** Every 3D thing in Screens v3 — the islands, buildings, terrain features, bridges, skiffs, skies and all 43 characters — is generated by JavaScript at run time from three.js primitives and signed-distance sculpts. Nothing is a hand-made mesh file. That matches §21.11 ("the world is generated in code") and means the exact models can be reproduced on demand.
- **Recommended pipeline: export, don't port, the fixed assets.** Run the prototype's own modules headless and write each creature (mesh, vertex paint with baked occlusion, skeleton, skin weights, the 15 clips sampled at 30 fps, expressions, effect sockets) and each building, prop and terrain-feature piece to .glb with three.js's GLTFExporter. I did this for Featherkin: a 1.73 MB .glb that Godot 4.5 imports as one `Skeleton3D` of 24 bones, 6 `BoneAttachment3D`s for the rigid accessories, 101 meshes with vertex colours and an `AnimationPlayer` of 15 animations whose 435 tracks all resolve. **Port to GDScript only what must follow the simulation**: the island base around the sim's square grid, tiles, heights, surfaces, feature and building placement, scatter, bridges, characters' positions and clip choice, overlays, and the shaders.
- **Nothing about the look survives the export except geometry and colour.** The toon ramp, the ink outline, the rim light, the down shells, the glow sprites, the particle points, the CSS sky and the x-ray silhouettes are three.js material patches, sprites or HTML, and are rebuilt as Godot shaders and nodes (section 5). One trap: the prototype's outlines are back-face shells, and GLTFExporter writes back-face materials as ordinary front faces, so exported outlines would cover the models in black; the harness must strip them.
- **The designs are far over §21.3's budgets.** Measured: a realm view draws 163,000–219,000 triangles in 1,149–1,264 draw calls, the heist 225,558 in 940, the universe map 676,192 in 1,207 (§21.3: a realm 25,000–40,000, under 150 draw calls). Characters are 10,408–181,534 triangles at hero resolution and still 2,880–76,096 at the diorama's game resolution, in 15–233 meshes each (§21.3: 3,000–6,000). Buildings are inside budget (1,172–2,556). The export is where budgets are enforced: capping segment counts in the harness and coarsening the sculpt brought Featherkin from 47,644 to 12,576 without touching the prototype's code; reaching 6,000 needs the cape redrawn as fewer pieces.
- **Four things the design document says the sheets have are not in the 3D.** No sheet's 3D implements the guard form ("As a guard" only changes a label); Hush is a colour-only recolour, which §21.6 calls a failed form; no form adds an accessory; and several kinds' models are taller than their stated heights at their own game scale (Prism Mite 0.76 m against 0.5 m). §23.5 says all of these match.
- **A replay cannot yet be drawn with the right creatures**: the heist record, the island and the replay API name crew and guards by role only.
- **Proposed build order** (section 8): an art export harness with budget checks; the toon shader kit; a `World` 3D scene that reads `RulebornReplay` with 1 tile = 1 m; characters with forms, the guard form and springs; then performance on the reference device. Roughly 16–25 agent runs, none of which §26.8 or `tools/roadmap.py` budgets today.

## 2. What the client draws today

`client/scripts/realm_view.gd` is a `Control` that draws one realm flat and top-down with `_draw()` from the simulation's own state after each tick (`realm_view.gd:1–14` says so: "Top-down and flat for now: §21.1's diorama camera is the art pass"). It reads `RulebornReplay`'s `size()`, `features()`, `heights()`, `surfaces()`, `guards()`, `crew()`, `wards()`, `vault()` and `seen(i)` (`client/ext/src/lib.rs:286–418`), fits the grid to the rect with one tile of rim (`layout()`, `realm_view.gd:99–104`), and draws:

- ground per tile, darkened per height level, gravel and water with ink ripples (`:118–141`);
- the eight sim features as rectangles and lines (`_draw_feature`, `:161–182`);
- wards as their school's shape (`:187–228`), the Vault as a square (`:231–237`);
- guards as circles with a facing line, a state mark ("?", "!") and, for one selected guard, the tiles `seen(i)` returns filled in alarm red — sharp tile edges, as §21.12 asks (`:240–257`);
- crew as role-coloured circles with role letters and a carried-loot arc (`:260–274`);
- a screen-reader description (`describe`, `:54–66`), and `tile_at` for input (`:292–300`).

`client/scripts/heist_hud.gd` is the top row of the heist HUD in plain `Label`s: clock, alarm pips, loot (`:33–51`).

The project runs Godot **4.5** with the **Compatibility** renderer on mobile (`client/project.godot:12, 26–27`); v3.6 §24 pins 4.7.2-stable and api-4-7 (the shell report's finding P already covers the pin).

What this gives the 3D world for free: the simulation's tiles, heights (0–3, `sim/src/grid.rs:275`), surfaces (plain, gravel, water, moss, `grid.rs:90–95`), features (`grid.rs:116–136`), positions in tiles as floats, guard facing, cone and range, crew gait and standing, and the per-tile sight sets. That is the truth the diorama must draw. What it lacks is identity (section 6.3).

## 3. How Screens v3 builds its 3D

### 3.1 The realm diorama (`realm_diorama.js`)

**One custom element, six modes.** `<realm-diorama mode=… realm=…>` is used by the home (`universe`), realm view (`realm`), scout report, defence curation, rehearsal and terrain editor (`scout`, with a `defence` JSON for the last three), planner and landing picker (`plan`), and the heist (`heist`) (`screens_main.markup.html:367, 471, 678, 745, 772, 783`; `screens_meta.markup.html:219, 239, 433, 453, 473, 491`). Each instance creates its own WebGL context and rebuilds its whole scene on every mode change (`rebuild`, diorama:1053).

**Renderer and light** (`setup`, diorama:481–507): `WebGLRenderer` with antialiasing, a 2,048² PCF shadow map, sRGB output and **no tone mapping**; a `PerspectiveCamera` (28° field on the map, 20° in a realm and heist; `placeCam`, :512–516) orbiting at a 45° azimuth with device-tilt parallax; a `HemisphereLight`, one shadow-casting `DirectionalLight` "sun" and a pink back "rim" `DirectionalLight`; linear `Fog`. Per-realm light colours, intensities and sun positions are a table (`lights`, :1056); palettes per realm are another (`REALMS`, :60–67). A frame-time watchdog lowers the pixel ratio from 2 to 1.5 when frames average over 30 ms (:511).

**Materials** (:519–533): every solid is `MeshToonMaterial` with a **four-step** gradient map (`DataTexture` [62, 140, 215, 255], :485) and every colour pushed **+10% saturation** (`cc.offsetHSL(0,.1,0)`, :519). Outlines are **inverted hulls**: a child mesh sharing the geometry, scaled up, drawn with a back-face `MeshBasicMaterial` in `#140A18` (:486, :520), so their width is in world units, not pixels. Emissive windows and crystals use the toon material's emissive. Glows are additive `Sprite`s with a radial canvas texture (`glow`, :521). Textures are drawn on canvases at start-up: a grass-stroke texture, a rock-strata texture, water, waterfall, lava, path, rune and cone textures (:491–503, :545, :551, :568, :918, :1297–1306), several with `Math.random`, so they differ every load.

**Draw-call reduction already in the prototype**: `merge` (:523–528) collapses every static, untextured, non-emissive toon mesh of an island into **one vertex-coloured mesh**; `mergeLocal` (:687–690) does the same per building; `mergeAcc` (:590–599) merges a character's accessories per bone and material; scatter uses `InstancedMesh` (:901, :919–920).

**The island** (`makeIsland`, :945–977): a floating disc of radius 12.7 × IK with a noisy edge (`EF`, five sines, :948); a top surface built as a **polar grid of 62 rings × 220 spokes** (about 27,000 triangles) with vertex colours from two sine-noise fields, a rim swell (`hAt`, :952) and an accent tint (:953–957), textured with the grass canvas; three rim bands and an inverted, jittered rock cone underneath with the strata texture (:958–962); hanging roots as tubes, grass fringes, rocks and vines along the rim (`detail`, :881–911); 1,500 instanced grass tufts plus pebbles and flowers (:907–911); seeded `rngF(hs(k))` for most placement but `Math.random` in others. Rivers and lava are ribbon meshes along Catmull-Rom curves with scrolling textures and banks, ending in waterfall planes and mist sprites (:542–570, :1002–1009); birds, clouds, floating rocks, motes are small animated groups (:571, :1016–1048).

**The tile grid is a circle whose size changes by mode.** `TILES` is every integer cell within radius 10.2 × IK (:92), and IK is 2.05 in realm and scout modes, 1.4 in the heist and 1 on the map (:1053). One tile is one unit, so the realm view's island holds about 1,370 tiles (some 42 across) and the heist's about 640 (some 28 across). The defence/rehearsal/terrain overlay instead lays a **9 × 9 grid of 3.9-unit cells** turned 45° over the same island (`DF.S=3.9`, :280), and draws height levels as 2.1-unit blocks (`HL=2.1`, :308) — 0.54 of a cell per level.

**Buildings**: `makeB2` (:691–748) builds the realm-view versions of Hearth, Star Well, "Aether Loom", Garden, Guard Post and Vault from many small boxes, cylinders, tori and prisms (windows with shutters, doors with arches, timber frames, roof shingles, a working well wheel, a loom wheel, a greenhouse), then merges them; `makeB` (:756–880) builds lighter versions for the map plus the Ward and Spire; `castle` (:638–664) and `walls` (:665–671) build the Heart's citadel; `signature`/`topper`/`dress` (:679–686, :749–755, :981–987) add a per-realm topper, a lamp post, a flag at level 3 and banners and a finial at level 6. The full building is the same at every level (measured: identical counts at levels 1, 5 and 9, `3dp/out/kit.json`).

**Terrain features**: the defence overlay's `feat` switch (:316–365) builds a small group for each of `content/terrain.json`'s 39 features (stream, pond, marsh, ice, rapids, hot spring, waterfall, tall grass, bush, hedge, tree, flowers, thicket, vines, bounce mushroom, boulder, gravel, scree, pillar, crystal, lantern, canopy, fog, updraft vent, ember vent, lava crack, static stone, fence, statue, ruin, bell post, ramp, stairs, cliff, pit, arch, tunnel, stepping stones, plus raised ground as lift blocks), each with a small per-frame animation (:366–376). This is a complete terrain kit, drawn at 3.9-unit cells.

**Links between islands** (`link`, :1167–1195): stone bridge (arched blocks and parapets), rope bridge (planks, rope tubes, swaying), chute (a half-pipe of 60 × 10 quads with scrolling water) and rift (two portal rings with a stream of glows), parametric on the gap. **Skiffs** (`makeSkiff`, :1196–1209; `skiffs`, :1214–1223) are a lathe hull, mast, a vertex-curved sail and glow trails.

**Sky and screen effects are HTML, not 3D**: the canvas is transparent over a CSS gradient `div` per realm (:1057–1059) or a galaxy for the map (:1074); a CSS radial **vignette** and, on the map, a CSS **backdrop-filter blur** masked to the top and bottom quarters (a tilt-shift; :116); a CSS cloud wipe between realms (`cloudWipe`, :1011–1012). The map's depth comes from 700 + 160 + 120 star `Points`, nebula and galaxy planes, comets and debris (:1076–1134). Pins, labels, HP bars, pips, the VAULT tag and pop-up numbers are absolutely positioned HTML moved each frame by projecting world points (`proj`; e.g. :241–254, :449, :457).

**Law effects** (`lawFX`, :1312–1332) exist for nine laws: Stillness (a clock-face plaza), Slow rivers, Half gravity (floating rocks), Current (streaks), Glass (glass boxes over buildings), Day and night (a 30 s light cycle), Frictionless (ice decals), Bloom (hedges and flowers) and Glowing dark. §21.9 budgets 24.

**Characters in the diorama** are *not* the sculpted heroes. `makeChar0` (:611–628) builds a 24-mesh chibi (sphere body, eyes, a kind-specific top: ears, round ears, bear ears, antenna, flame or horn; feet; arms; a random tool, sack or crate), 2,226 triangles, scaled 1.9–2.3. Workers, guards and the heist crew all use it; guards are its "glass" kind with a cone helmet and a lantern glow for every role (:143). The sculpts are wired in (`heroLoad`/`upHero`/`heroTick`, :579–610: load the module, build at resolution 0.014–0.019, cache in IndexedDB, merge accessories, drop fur shells, add an x-ray silhouette with `depthFunc: GreaterDepth`, drive idle/walk/run/ko by speed) but only when `window.RB_HEROES` is set, which nothing in the artifact sets. When they do load, every kind is scaled to the same 0.64–0.76 units × 2.2 (:602), ignoring each module's `GAME_SCALE`.

**The heist mode runs its own little simulation** (`buildHeist`/`hUpdate`, :122–238: BFS paths, cones of 45°/40°/35° with ranges 2/4/6, suspicion rates and thresholds, grabs for 90 damage, revives, a skiff). None of it is to be ported: in the build the heist is the Rust simulation's (§24.7), and the 3D only draws `RulebornReplay`. The heist report covers its rules.

### 3.2 The creature kit (`creatures/creature_kit.js` and the 43 modules)

The kit's own header states the method: "SDF clay volume -> surface nets -> painted + baked AO -> fur shells -> skinned rig" (kit:2). In detail:

1. **Sculpt.** Each module's `sculpt()` places named primitives: ellipsoids `E(name, centre, radii, rotation, blend)` and round cones `C(name, a, b, ra, rb, blend)`, unioned with a smooth minimum (blend radius per primitive), or subtracted (`op 's'`, e.g. Featherkin's eye sockets, `featherkin.js:18`) (kit:15–30). A `disp()` adds surface displacement — Featherkin's feather scallops are a displacement pattern (`featherkin.js:37`). The primitive's name before the colon is the bone it belongs to (`'shL:wing1'`, `'T:belly'` for torso).
2. **Mesh.** The signed-distance field is sampled on a regular grid at step `res` over the module's box and meshed by **surface nets** (one vertex per sign-changing cell, quads across sign-changing edges), with normals from the field's gradient (kit:31–46). Featherkin at its hero step 0.0078 is a 130 × 145 × 94 grid, 1.77 million field evaluations, 3.5 s in desktop Chromium.
3. **Paint.** Per vertex: ambient occlusion by marching five steps along the normal through the field, a warm tint into the occlusion, then the module's `paint()` — pattern by position, normal and distance to named parts (feather barbs, facial disc, belly bars) — plus value noise; stored as **vertex colours**, with a `furMask` attribute (kit:48–55). No textures on bodies.
4. **Material.** `MeshPhysicalMaterial` with vertex colours, roughness 0.8, **sheen**, and a shader patch (`rimize`, kit:62) that adds a side-masked Fresnel rim and a warm wrap term; optional emissive from vertex colour (`vcGlow`, kit:71). **Fur shells**: N copies of the body pushed out along the normal in the vertex shader and cut by a hash noise in the fragment shader (kit:64–66, 72). Seven kinds use shells: Hourkeeper 2, Tidewalker 5, Mossbear 6, Rambler, Soulmender, Sparkwright and Stormhound 7 each; Featherkin's are off (`shells:0`, `featherkin.js:8`) although its sheet's caption mentions down shells.
5. **Accessories.** Separate meshes from three.js primitives — eyes (sphere, iris decal from a canvas texture, lids, catch-lights; `creature_parts.js:19–31`), markings as tubes projected onto the surface by ray-marching the field (`featherkin.js:56, 66`), straps, buckles, binoculars, the cape — in `MeshPhysicalMaterial` with clearcoat, metalness (brass), transmission (glass), iridescence. Each names the bone it rides on (`userData.bone`).
6. **Rig** (`makeRigKit`, kit:86–130). A joint table `J` (name → parent, rest position). **Skin weights are computed**: each vertex goes to the bone of the nearest named primitive; torso vertices blend between torso segments by distance; five passes of neighbour smoothing; the top four weights kept (kit:88–96). Accessories are re-parented to their bones as rigid children (kit:104). `AIM` straightens chains to an A-pose; feet are put on the floor (kit:107–111).
7. **Pose model.** A pose is a small object: root offset (`x, y, z`), turn and lean (`ry, rx, rz`), squash (`sq`, a non-uniform scale on a `lean` group), head and ear angles, eye open and width (eye meshes' scale), and per-bone Euler offsets `b:{bone:[x,y,z]}` (kit:118–128). On top, `set()` adds **springs** — secondary motion of ears, tail and cape driven by the root's vertical speed, turn rate, forward speed or a sine of time (`cfg.springs`, e.g. `featherkin.js:100`) — an **arm lag** spring, timed **spins** and **effect nodes** that grow, show or hide with a pose value (kit:113–128). `play()` cross-fades between clips over 280 ms (kit:117).
8. **Clips.** `stdClips` (kit:132–148) defines 14 procedural clips as pure functions of time — idle, idle 2, walk, run, carry, climb, leap, slide, wade, hit, knocked out, celebrate, nap, work — built from a parametric gait (kit:80–84), tuned by body weight and arm swing; each module overrides some and adds its **ability** clip (Featherkin's glide, `featherkin.js:102–113`), making 15. Four **expressions** (curious, focused, startled, caught) are static poses (`EXPR`, `featherkin.js:114–118`). `ROUTE` scripts the "in game" walk on the sheet (`featherkin.js:122`). `GAME_SCALE` converts sculpt units to metres (`featherkin.js:120`, 0.62).
9. **Special pipelines.** Skyskipper and the Archivist have their own sculpt and rig files in the same style (`skyskipper-sculpt.js`, `skyskipper-rig.js`, `archivist-sculpt.js`, `archivist-rig.js`); moths and puffballs share bases (`moth_base.js`, `puffball_base.js`); the Sages share `sage_kit.js` (face, cloth shading); Twinned is two bodies on one 38-bone rig; the Illusory guard re-exports Featherkin (`illusoryguard.js:5`). The modules reference `.bin` files of pre-baked meshes (`BIN`), but no `.bin` is in the bundle, so every page meshes its creature live and caches it in IndexedDB.

**Featherkin's cape** is 59 feathers in five staggered rows of 9, 11, 12, 13 and 14 (`featherkin.js:71–72`): each row's feather is one `ExtrudeGeometry` (a bevelled leaf shape, curved, vertex-coloured from gold to cream), and each feather is its **own mesh** sharing its row's geometry — not instanced — rotated around the back with a little jitter and bound rigidly to one of three cape bones (`capeL`, `capeC`, `capeR`) by its angle. The cape moves by those three bones and their springs. At hero resolution the accessories are 34,924 triangles in 100 meshes; by my count of their primitives roughly 14,000 are the cape and about as many the two eyes (40-segment spheres, irises, lids and rings), the rest the binoculars and straps. With segment counts capped (section 4.1) the eyes shrink to a few hundred and the cape dominates.

## 4. Two ways into Godot, compared

### 4.1 Export the prototype's own models (what I ran)

The harness (`3dp/site/measure.html`, `exportKind`) does, per kind:

1. Seed `Math.random` (the prototype calls it in places), import the module, `build({res, shells: 0})`, `makeRig`.
2. Give every node a unique name so animation tracks can bind.
3. Replace `performance.now` with a fake clock and, for each clip, run one loop to settle the springs, then record one loop at 30 fps: every node's position, rotation and scale, keeping only nodes that move; build three.js `AnimationClip`s.
4. Write a binary glTF with `GLTFExporter`.

Result for Featherkin at the diorama's game resolution: **1,728,980 bytes** (geometry 1.18 MB as 32-bit floats, animation 272 KB, two textures 79 KB), 133 nodes, 1 skin of 24 joints, 47 meshes, 22 materials, 15 animations of 8–23 channels each (`3dp/out/featherkin@0.019.glb`). At hero resolution: 4,301,556 bytes. Export took 0.2–0.3 s after a 0.5–4.5 s build.

**Godot 4.5 imported it** (`3dp/godot_probe/probe.gd`, `probe2.gd`): one `Skeleton3D` with the 24 bones by name, 6 `BoneAttachment3D` nodes carrying the rigid accessories, 101 `MeshInstance3D`s (47,644 triangles, matching three.js), vertex colours on 60 surfaces, all materials as `StandardMaterial3D`, and an `AnimationPlayer` with the 15 animations (lengths 0.43–5.00 s) whose 435 tracks (15 position, 375 rotation, 45 scale — the scale tracks are squash and blinks) **all resolve**. The imported scene is 1.04 MB with Godot's default mesh compression and generated LODs.

What survives: geometry, vertex paint with its baked occlusion, the bone hierarchy and weights, rigid accessories on their bones, every clip as keyframes (including squash, blinks, ear and cape springs as they play in place), canvas textures (written as PNG), node names. Godot maps sheen and clearcoat only partly; that does not matter, because §21.3 replaces these materials (section 5).

What does not survive, and must be handled:

- **Shader patches** (`onBeforeCompile`: rim, wrap, fur shells, vertex-colour glow) — rebuilt as Godot shaders.
- **Outline hulls**: GLTFExporter writes `doubleSided` only for `DoubleSide` (`GLTFExporter.js:1734` in r184), so the prototype's back-face outline meshes would arrive as front-facing black shells covering the model. Strip them (`material === olMat`) before export.
- **Sprites** (glows, embers, mist): GLTFExporter writes only meshes, lines and points (`GLTFExporter.js:2428`). Export each as an empty node (a socket) carrying colour and size in its name or extras, and rebuild as billboards or particles.
- **`InstancedMesh`**: exported with `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` marked required, and **Godot 4.5 refuses the whole file**: "required extension 'EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing' is not supported" (`3dp/godot_probe/import_island2.log`). Export one instance and its placements, and build a `MultiMeshInstance3D` (check whether 4.7.2 reads the extension; the placement route works either way).
- **`userData`** with circular references breaks the exporter (the diorama's buildings point back to their groups); strip it as `makeRig`'s own `stripUD` does (kit:97).
- **Springs driven by turning and moving**: baking in place keeps bob-driven springs but loses the ears and cape swinging when the creature turns or starts (kit:119 reads the parent's rotation). Use Godot's `SpringBoneSimulator3D` (added in 4.4) on the ear, tail and cape chains, tuned from each module's `cfg.springs`, and bake the clips with springs off.
- **Per-frame vertex animation** (flags and sails waving, the planner's flag; diorama :740, :754, :468) becomes a vertex shader on `TIME`.
- **The custom `furMask` attribute** arrives as `_FURMASK`; put it in the vertex colour's alpha channel at export so a Godot shader can read it.

Budgets are enforced in the export, without editing the prototype: a `three` shim that caps segment counts of the prototype's own primitive calls (`3dp/site/three-lod.js`) plus a coarser sculpt step gave:

To reach §21.3's 3,000–6,000 the accessory-heavy kinds need art changes the harness cannot make: Featherkin's 59 feathers as a handful of skinned cards, Mossbear's and the Gardener's moss and leaves as vertex paint rather than geometry. Merging all rigid accessories into the body as one skinned, vertex-coloured mesh (weight 1 to their bone; textured eyes kept separate) takes a character to 2–3 draw calls.

## 6. The diorama in Godot, on the simulation's tiles

### 6.1 One world, one scale

A `World` scene (`Node3D`) owned by the app shell, built once and kept across screens — the prototype re-creates a WebGL context and rebuilds the scene on every mode change (:121, :1053), which Godot should not copy. Its layers:

1. **Island**: the realm's base, generated around the sim's grid (`RulebornReplay.size()`, 10 × 10 to 14 × 14, `sim/src/grid.rs:57`), keeping the prototype's round rock and rim but sized so the square grid sits inside it (the shell report's finding E).
2. **Tiles**: 1 tile = 1 m. Tile (x, y) of the sim is the box from (x, 0, y) to (x + 1, h × H, y + 1) in metres, centred on the island; `RulebornReplay` positions are already in tiles as floats (`client/ext/src/lib.rs:346, 364`), so a raider at (3.5, 7.25) stands at X = 3.5 − w/2, Z = 7.25 − h/2. Heights 0–3 are raised blocks (the prototype's lift blocks, :311–315); H, the height of one level, is not in the document (finding 10). Surfaces tint the tile top; water uses the water shader.
3. **Features**: one exported kit piece per `content/terrain.json` feature, placed per tile; route-changing ("hero") features drawn individually and ambient ones as texture (§21.10).
4. **Buildings**: one exported look per building and level band, placed from the island once buildings have footprints in the island format (the shell report's finding N); merged per building.
5. **Actors**: crew, guards and workers instanced from the kind .glb files.
6. **Overlays**: the selected guard's seen tiles as a `MultiMesh` of flat quads (one draw call), drawn with hard edges from `replay.seen(i)` exactly as `realm_view.gd:246–249` does today (§21.12); other guards' facing chevrons; routes; rings; labels and pips as `Control`s placed with `Camera3D.unproject_position`.
7. **Camera**: orthographic, 35° pitch at home and 50° in the heist, rotation in 90° steps (§21.1; the shell and heist reports cover the camera).
8. **Light and sky** per realm from the prototype's tables.

### 6.2 Motion from the simulation

The simulation steps 30 times a second (§24); the screen draws 60. Keep the previous and current tick's positions and facing for every actor and interpolate by the fraction of a tick elapsed, so motion is smooth without the view ever leading the simulation. Clip choice is a pure function of the tick's state: `gait` (walk, run, crouch), `standing` (down → knocked out, caught, out), `carried > 0` → carry, an ability command this tick → ability; guards' `state` → alert or idle; `down` → knocked out. Clip time comes from ticks, not wall-clock, so a replay looks the same every time it is played — which the 15-second clips of §24 need. No `Math.random`-style choices in the view (the prototype picks a worker's tool at random, :625).

`RealmView` stays: it is the screen-reader description, the fallback on devices that fail the 3D check, and the cheapest golden test (positions and seen tiles can be asserted without a GPU).

### 6.3 What the view needs that the replay does not give

The 3D draws ruleborn, not roles. Today a raider and a guard are a role and a position (`sim/src/lib.rs:447, 584`; `client/ext/src/lib.rs:341–373`), the heist record holds a crew count (`sim/src/replay.rs:147`), and a post is a row and a tile (`sim/src/island.rs:87–91`). Finding 6.

## 7. Performance on the reference device

§24: "A mid-range 4 GB Android at 60 Hz, not a flagship"; "60 frames a second is a floor"; "Under 25 ms from touch to picture for dragging". §21.3: under 150 draw calls; realm 25,000–40,000 triangles; characters 3,000–6,000; buildings 2,000–8,000.

A plan that fits, per view (estimates, to be measured on a phone in TC.8):

| Item | Triangles | Draw calls |
|---|---|---|
| Island base (polar grid cut to ~24 × 120, rim, rock) | 6,000–8,000 | 3–4 (merged per material) |
| Tiles, heights and surfaces | 2,000–4,000 | 2–4 (`MultiMesh` per kind) |
| Scatter (tufts, pebbles, flowers; far fewer than the prototype's 2,210) | 3,000–6,000 | 3–5 (`MultiMesh`) |
| Features (hero ones individually) | 2,000–5,000 | 10–20 |
| 7–12 buildings, merged | 14,000–25,000 | 10–24 |
| Characters: 5 crew + 1–3 guards + workers, LOD at realm zoom | 15,000–40,000 | 2–3 each plus outline: 25–60 |
| Overlays, glows, particles | 1,000–3,000 | 10–20 |
| **Total** | **43,000–91,000** | **63–137** |

So draw calls fit under 150 with merging, `MultiMesh` and one or two surfaces per character; triangles fit 40,000 only at realm zoom with character LODs, not in the heist (finding 8). Fill rate, not triangles, is the risk on a mid-range phone at 1080 × 2340: render the 3D at 0.75 scale (Compatibility offers bilinear scaling only; check 4.7.2), keep additive glows few, no full-screen passes, outlines on characters only.

The 25 ms drag budget is a separate problem: at 60 Hz one frame is 16.7 ms, and Godot plus Android's compositor normally hold at least one more frame between reading a touch and showing it (finding 9).

**9. The 25 ms drag budget is not achievable as written on a 60 Hz phone, and its measurement is undefined (gap, significant).** §24: "Under 25 ms from touch to picture for dragging". One 60 Hz frame is 16.7 ms; Godot renders the frame after the touch is read and the Android compositor presents it at the next refresh, so a drag drawn from the latest touch typically appears two or more frames later. Neither §24 nor any test says how the 25 ms is measured, whether prediction counts, or what a failure triggers. Recommend: define it as measured on the reference device with a high-speed camera; allow drag extrapolation of one to two frames; read touches as late as possible (unaccumulated input) and keep the 3D frame under about 8 ms so nothing else queues; add it to TC.8.
